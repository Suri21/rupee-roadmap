A step-by-step money plan

Plan your money in the order a good adviser would

Most people start by asking where to invest. That is the last of four questions. Answer them in order and you will know what each rupee is for, where it should go, and whether it gets you there.

Protect before you grow

An emergency fund and insurance stop one bad month from undoing years of investing.

Every rupee needs a job and a date

Goals decide how much to invest. Time to the goal decides where.

Judge a plan by its range

Look at what you'd have in a weak market and in today's money, not just the best-case number.

Step 2 of 4

What am I saving for?

List every goal, big or small. You'll see what each one will really cost after inflation, how much to invest for it every month, and how far short you are in total.

A
Adviser's note

"I should save more" never works. "₹7,500 a month for 16 years for my daughter's college" does. When you can't fund everything, protect retirement first: there are loans for education, but none for retirement.

Step 3 of 4

How should I invest for each goal?

Answer five questions to find your risk profile. Each goal then gets a mix of investment types based on how soon you need the money, what kind of goal it is, and how much risk you can take.

A
Adviser's note

Where to invest depends on two things: when you need the money, and how big a fall you can sit through without selling. Money you need within 3 years stays out of equity, however good recent returns look.

Step 4 of 4

Will I get there?

Pick a goal and see how much you'd have at the end with a mix of investments. Change the monthly amount, the yearly increase or the mix, and the result updates.

A
Adviser's note

No single instrument gets you there. A mix grows faster than FDs alone and falls less than equity alone. Look at the weak-market number as well as the expected one, and remember to read the result in today's money.
